Day 1

It starts as an email

Because it always does. The brief arrives with a deck attached and a shoot window buried in the third paragraph.

One click files it against the job, with who sent it and when. Six months later that is still the answer to where the brief came from.

Day 3

The boards go up

Frames in order, each with its scene, its duration and its notes. Built here, or pulled straight out of Figma.

The references sit beside them rather than in a folder called refs_new_2.

Day 6

The shoot

The call sheet is built on the sheet itself, not in a form that generates one. Send it as a private link per person.

Then it tells you the thing a PDF never could: who has actually confirmed, and who has only opened it.

Day 12

The cut goes to the client

A link. No login, no account, no seat to buy for someone who will use it twice.

Comments land on the frame, at the timecode, with the markup drawn on it. You reply in the same place.

Day 15

Approved, with a name on it

Three rounds, all kept. v1 is still there, and so is every comment that changed it.

The approval is a record, not a sentence in a thread you will be searching for on a Thursday.

Day 18

What it actually cost

Vendor invoices arrive by email and file themselves against a budget line, amount read off the PDF.

Kessler came in $300 over the agreed rate, so it says so. Deposits and balances are tracked apart, because they are paid apart.

Day 22

And this is the point

Not any one of those screens. The fact that twenty-two days of a job sit on one surface, and will still be there in a year when the client asks for the cutdown.

No archaeology. No eleven tabs. No folder called FINAL_v3.

Also in there, off the path this job took

Leads and deals with a pipeline, client and contact records, agreements and signed proposals, a shareable batch review for picking between takes, the generated-shot pipeline with its provenance kept, connections to Gmail, Drive, Figma, Slack and Calendar, and an assistant that proposes changes as a card and never once makes them itself.

Run a real job through it

Not a trial account you poke at for ten minutes. One job, brief to delivery, with your real client and your real deadline. That is the only test that tells either of us anything.
