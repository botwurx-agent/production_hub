The version that went out anyway

The balance that aged quietly

The deposit landed. The balance did not. Nothing in the pile was watching for it, so it surfaced six weeks late.

None of this is a discipline problem. Producers are the most organised people on a job. It is a filing problem, and filing is what software is for.

The stance

Organise, do not replace

Nobody is going to abandon Figma because a project tool asked them to. So Studio Flows reaches into where the work already lives and files it against the job.

Gmail threads, replies, attachments Google Drive browse and import Figma frames as assets Slack channels Google Chat spaces Calendar two way

The test for any connection is simple: it has to remove a tool switch that happens on every job. Anything else is a logo on a page.

Where AI sits

It proposes. You commit.

“Log the Kessler invoice against lighting, half now and half on delivery.”

Ask it in your own words. It reads the studio and comes back with a card, not a change. Nothing is written until you press Create.

That line is not caution for its own sake. A model that files a cost unattended will eventually file the wrong one, and a financial record you did not agree to is worse than no record. Same rule everywhere it appears: it drafts the client update, it reads the invoice, it rewrites the reply. You send it.

Why it exists

Every feature came from something that went wrong

Studio Flows is built by a working commercial studio operator, and used on live jobs before it is shown to anyone.

There is no roadmap workshop behind it. The backlog is a list of things that got in the way last week: an approval that could not be found, an invoice that arrived as a photograph, a crew rate a freelancer should never have been able to see. When a job runs clean, nothing gets built. That is the rule, and it is why the product does not sprawl.

It is also why the design is warm rather than white. On cold white the interface is the brightest thing on screen, and the work has to fight it. That matters when the work is the thing being judged.

The ask

Run a real job through it

Not a trial account you poke at for ten minutes. One job, brief to delivery, with your real client and your real deadline. That is the only test that tells either of us anything.
