SF Studio Flows Brand foundation

Built on paper, not on glare

Studio Flows is where a commercial job lives. The brand has one job: to look like it was made by someone who has actually sat through a colour session, and not by a software company.

Everything here already exists in the product as design tokens. This sheet fixes which of them the outward-facing surfaces use, so the deck, the marketing site and the app read as one thing. The rule that governs all of it: the ground is warm, the accent is rare, and colour means something.

The ground

Warm neutral, not white

The category is cold. Monday, ClickUp, Asana and Notion all sit on blue-white with saturated accents. Sitting on a warm ground is the cheapest, most durable way to not look like them, and it is the one choice a viewer registers before reading a single word.

It is also functional, which is what makes it defensible rather than decorative. This palette exists in the product as the paper theme, built for long review sessions because the brightness of the interface changes how you judge the work sitting inside it.

The warm ground stops competing with the image. On white, the interface is the brightest thing on screen and the frame has to fight it. That is a real problem when the frame is the thing being judged, and it is the sentence to use when anyone asks why the site does not look like every other tool.

The product ships light, paper and dark as three value sets for the same tokens. Outward-facing surfaces use paper. Light and dark remain in the app, chosen by the user, and dark is the right ground for a screenshot of a review session.

Colour as signal

Two colour systems that never mix

This is the rule most tools get wrong, and the one that keeps the interface calm as the product grows. Colour is either state or identity. It is never decoration, and the two never appear in the same role.

State: small, contained, always a chip

A tinted pill with a dot. It never floods a row or a card, because a full colour wash makes the whole screen feel urgent and then nothing does.

In review Changes requested Approved Overdue Delivered

Identity: wayfinding, never status

Each module carries a fixed hue so the eye learns where things are. These are never read as good or bad.

Indigo is for action only

The accent marks the primary button, the active state and the mark itself. If indigo appears anywhere it is not inviting a click, it is being used as decoration, and it should come out.

The problem

A job lives in eleven places

The brief is in email. The boards are in Figma. The cut is in Dropbox. The feedback is in a thread nobody can find by Thursday.

Voice

One operator talking to another

Write the way a producer talks on a Tuesday. Plain, specific, unhurried. Name the real thing: a call sheet is a call sheet, not a "resource document".

Say

  • Send a link. Your client needs no login.
  • Comments land on the frame, at the timecode.
  • Every version stays one thing, not nine files.

Never say

  • Platform, solution, streamline, seamless.
  • Empower, unlock, supercharge, revolutionise.
  • Anything a producer would not say out loud on set.

Rules

The short version

Do

  • Ground everything outward-facing in paper.
  • Keep indigo for actions and the mark.
  • Let type and whitespace carry the structure.
  • Show real product, real project names, real dates.
  • Set display type tight and heavy.

Do not

  • Put the brand on pure white.
  • Use a second accent, or a gradient as identity.
  • Colour a whole row or card by status.
  • Use stock photography of people in offices.
  • Centre body copy, or set display type loose.

This sheet is deliberately single-theme. It is a specimen of the paper ground, so inverting it would remove the thing it exists to show. Colour values are the live tokens in app/globals.css; if they change there, they change here.
