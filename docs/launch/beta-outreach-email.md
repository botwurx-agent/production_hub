# Beta outreach to colleagues

Two versions. Use the short one by default.

The audience is other production people, so this reads as one operator
showing another something they built, not as a product launch. Nothing here
uses the word "platform", "solution", or "streamline", because a producer who
reads those knows immediately they are being marketed to by someone who does
not do the job.

**Send these one at a time from your own address.** Not BCC, not a mail tool.
A personal first line naming the last thing you worked on together is worth
more than everything else in the email.

---

## Version A: short (recommended)

Subject options, in order of preference:

- `Built something for pre-pro, want your eyes on it`
- `Been building this for our side of the business`
- `Can I get you to break something for me?`

> Hi [name],
>
> [One personal line. What you last worked on together, or what they have
> coming up.]
>
> I have spent the last few months building something for our side of the
> business, and it is finally good enough to put in front of people.
>
> It is called Studio Flows. One place for everything a job throws off: the
> brief, the boards, the shot list, the call sheet, the cuts, client feedback,
> the budget, the contracts. Built around how a job actually runs, not as
> another generic project tool with our words pasted on it.
>
> The part I would most like you to hit is client review. You send a link, the
> client needs no login, they drop comments straight on the frame or at a
> timecode, mark up the image if they want, and approve or request changes.
> It lands back on the project instead of dying in an email thread.
>
> It is free while it is in beta, and I am only opening it to people whose
> opinion I actually want. If you have a job coming up, even a small one, run
> it through and tell me where it annoys you. That is genuinely the useful
> part.
>
> Want me to send you a login?
>
> [you]

**Why this shape.** The ask is a yes or no question, not a signup. One feature
is named specifically so it is concrete, and it is the one that survives a
demo. "Tell me where it annoys you" gives them a job they can actually do and
signals you want criticism, which is the difference between a reply and a
polite nothing.

## Version B: longer, with the breakdown

Use when someone has already said "sounds interesting, what does it do", or
for the one or two peers you know read everything.

> Hi [name],
>
> [Personal line.]
>
> Short version: I got tired of a job living in eleven places, so I built the
> thing I wanted. It is called Studio Flows, it is live, and I am letting a
> few people in free while it is in beta.
>
> The idea is that a job has one home, and the home is shaped like production
> rather than like a database. Here is what is in it.
>
> **The job**
> Every project has a hub: brief, assets, boards, review, contacts, calendar,
> call sheets, budget, documents, contracts. Board, list, and a studio-wide
> timeline so you can see every job's shoot and delivery dates in one view and
> catch two shoots landing on the same day.
>
> **Creative and review**
> Assets with real version history, so v1 through v9 stay one thing instead of
> nine files. Storyboards, shot lists, and moodboards built in.
>
> Review is the part I care most about. Internally your team comments and
> signs off. Externally you send a link, no account needed. On a still they
> pin a comment to the exact spot and can draw on it. On a cut they comment at
> a timecode, or across a range for "this whole section drags", and clicking
> the note plays that stretch back. Threaded replies, reactions, filters,
> crop guides for checking a 9:16 cutdown survives, and a response-by date
> that chases them for you if it passes.
>
> **Shoot**
> Call sheets you build on the sheet itself rather than in a form, with
> templates, and per-person links so you can see who opened it and who
> confirmed. A contacts roster per job split into crew, talent, extras,
> vendors and client, with day rates. Project calendar for the dates a job
> actually has.
>
> **Money**
> Budget with bid against actual, and actuals backed by a cost ledger rather
> than a number you typed. Log a vendor invoice and it holds the invoice
> itself, what is owed, and when. Deposits and payment schedules, so "25 up
> front, balance on delivery" is one commitment with two payments. Margin
> against what you actually billed. Estimates, proposals your client signs in
> the browser, and invoices, delivered by email, link, or PDF.
>
> **Paperwork**
> Somewhere for permits, insurance certificates, licences and delivery specs,
> versioned, and tagged with the email they arrived in, so "where did that PDF
> go" has an answer. NDAs and MSAs live on the client and SOWs live on the
> job, tracking both signatures and flagging an NDA about to lapse.
>
> **Clients**
> Accounts, contacts, and a deal pipeline, because the same brand comes back
> with a different job and the old way of tracking that was a spreadsheet.
> Activity timeline, tasks, and linked email threads so "when did we last talk
> to them" is answerable.
>
> **Connects to what you already use**
> Gmail, Google Drive, Google Calendar, Slack and Figma. Link an email thread
> to a job and reply from inside it. Pull frames from a Figma file or files
> from Drive straight in as assets. The idea is to stop you tab-switching, not
> to make you abandon tools that work.
>
> **AI, only where it earns it**
> Nothing runs on its own and nothing sends on your behalf. Drop in a vendor
> invoice and it fills the cost form for you to check. Drop in a SOW and it
> pulls out the deliverables and fees. A polish button on a message you are
> already writing. Project summaries. There is also an assistant that reads
> the studio and answers questions, but it proposes changes and you confirm
> them; it never writes on its own.
>
> **If you do generated work**
> There is a whole pipeline for it: shots, references, candidate triage by
> keyboard, provenance on every generation, a prompt and style library, and a
> shareable link where a client picks their favourite from a set of takes.
> Organising the fan-out, not generating.
>
> It is free while it is in beta. Fair warning that it is early and built by
> one person, so expect rough edges and do not make it the only copy of
> anything that matters. What I need is someone running a real job through it
> and telling me where it gets in the way.
>
> Want a login?
>
> [you]

## Before you send

- **Take a login for yourself through the front door first.** You have only
  ever entered as the owner of an existing studio. Sign up cold and see what a
  new person sees, including whether the first-run checklist and the tour do
  their job on an empty account.
- **Invite peers as studio members, not project collaborators.** As of
  2026-07-31 the collaborator path (crew with access to one job only) has a
  verification in doubt, see CLAUDE.md. Studio members are the well-trodden
  path. Keep crew-level invites back until that is re-run.
- **Expect the first reply to be a question, not a signup.** That is fine and
  is the point of asking a yes or no question at the end.
- **Two or three people, not ten.** You want to watch what each one does, and
  the whole value of a beta this size is the detail you lose at volume.

## What to ask a week later

Not "what do you think", which gets "looks great". Ask:

- What did you open it for, and did you go back?
- Where did you go back to email or a spreadsheet instead?
- What did you look for and not find?

The third one is the backlog.
